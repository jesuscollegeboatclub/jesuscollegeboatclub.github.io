Leading the club

A word from the President

President 2025–26

Emma Tabernacle

Hi! I’m Emma, and I am the JCBC President. I’m a third-year Chemistry PhD student, and this will be my sixth year rowing with the club after learning as a novice during my undergraduate degree (apparently I never figured out how to leave).

Last year I had the privilege of serving as Lower Boats Captain for the second time, which has been one of the most rewarding parts of my time at Jesus. Highlights included captaining four boats in May Bumps, including the first ever W6 VIII, and seeing three novice crews enjoy a hugely successful Michaelmas term (we don’t talk about Emma Sprints). I’ve loved watching complete beginners gain confidence, make lifelong friends, and discover a sport that will become a defining part of their time in Cambridge.

One of the things that makes JCBC special is that, as the largest boat club on the Cam, we can offer opportunities for every level of rowing. Our first boats are consistently competitive, with several rowers trialling for CUBC each year, including people who first learnt to row at Jesus. At the same time, we’re just as proud of every novice’s first outing, every lower boat bump, and every personal milestone along the way, because every crew plays an important part in what makes the club so successful.

What has kept me at JCBC for six years isn’t just the rowing. The sense of community, the dedication of our coaches and committee, and the support of generations of alumni make this a very special club to be part of. I’m looking forward to building on that this year as we celebrate our bicentenary, support every crew, and make sure JCBC continues to be a place where everyone can thrive.

Women's Captain

Katy Bradley

Hi! I’m Katy, and I am the Women’s Captain for this exciting upcoming Bicentenary year. I learned to row on the Cam back in 2021, and have been physically unable to drag myself away since. I have absolutely loved becoming a part of JCBC over this last year — it has been a joy to get to know everyone and be inspired by such strong and determined athletes. Highlights have included defending both our Lents and May headships, fielding an incredible 6 women’s boats in the Mays, our annual training camp in Seville, and getting to race off-Cam, from the Tideway to the National Water Sports Centre in Nottingham.

As Women’s Captain, I have overall responsibility for the women’s squad, with special focus on the training and racing of the women’s first boat. I’m so excited to lead the squad this year as we build on the incredible success we’ve enjoyed in recent seasons and celebrate such a landmark year in the club’s history.

Whether you’re an experienced rower or have never stepped into a boat before, there’s a place for you at JCBC. Here’s to another year of chasing bumps, chasing 2k PBs, and occasionally chasing rowers who have slept through their alarms!

Coxing Captain

Arzoo Iqbal

I’m the loudest person in the boat, and arguably everywhere else too. As Coxing Captain, I’m responsible for looking after the club’s coxswains, from recruiting newcomers to supporting experienced coxes. One of the best parts of the role is watching people grow in confidence and become an integral part of their crew, and one of my main goals this year is to make coxing as welcoming and enjoyable as possible. There’s always something new to learn, no matter how much experience you have. With this year marking JCBC’s bicentenary, I’m especially excited to be part of such a significant chapter in the club’s history and can’t wait to see what our coxes and crews achieve, both on and off the water.
